# Scoring Rubric

Score each candidate item from 0 to 5 on seven dimensions, then compute a weighted total.

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Weight | Question |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Freshness | 1.0 | Is this new enough for the user's time window? |
| Source quality | 1.2 | Is the source primary, reputable, or consistently useful? |
| Audience fit | 1.4 | Does this matter to the chosen user persona? |
| Business impact | 1.3 | Could this affect strategy, revenue, investment, hiring, or positioning? |
| Novelty | 1.0 | Is this more than generic news everyone already knows? |
| Signal durability | 0.8 | Will this matter after the immediate news cycle fades? |
| Content potential | 1.1 | Can it become a useful post, memo, newsletter, or conversation starter? |

## Selection Rules

- Prefer items with high audience fit over broadly popular items.
- Prefer source-backed changes over vague predictions.
- Penalize duplicated news unless a second source adds new evidence.
- Penalize low-context hype even if it is viral.
- Penalize signals with high decay risk unless the user explicitly needs breaking news.
- Include at most one weak social/community signal unless it is verified by a stronger source.

## Score Interpretation

- `4.5+`: Lead signal. Put near the top.
- `3.8-4.4`: Strong candidate. Include if it adds diversity.
- `3.0-3.7`: Useful supporting item. Mention briefly or place under "watch next".
- `<3.0`: Usually omit.

## Tie Breakers

When scores are close, choose the item that:

1. Has a clearer source link.
2. Produces a sharper "why now".
3. Helps the user's stated purpose.
4. Adds a Korean or local-market angle when the user is Korea-focused.

## Signal Decay

Use signal durability to avoid overvaluing viral but short-lived items.

- `5`: Structural shift, platform change, regulation, durable customer behavior, or repeated funding/product pattern.
- `4`: Likely to matter for weeks or months, but needs more evidence.
- `3`: Useful this week, uncertain after that.
- `2`: Mostly headline-driven or tied to a one-off announcement.
- `1`: Viral item with little evidence or no clear follow-up path.
