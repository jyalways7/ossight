# Physical World Signal Rubric

Use this rubric when the user wants signals from spaces, objects, retail, food, hospitality, neighborhoods, pop-ups, exhibitions, home/living, or other material-world behavior.

The goal is not to imitate any public figure. The goal is to extract high-quality spatial and lifestyle judgment from public evidence.

## Observation Units

Start with scenes before naming trends:

- `Movement`: where people make the effort to go.
- `Dwell`: where people stay, wait, sit, browse, or repeat.
- `Queue`: what people spend time to access.
- `Payment`: what people pay for even when cheaper substitutes exist.
- `Photo`: what people choose to record and share.
- `Object`: what people carry, display, store, gift, or resell.
- `Route`: what places are bundled into one outing.
- `Video route`: what order creators show places, queues, entrances, tables, purchases, and photo spots.
- `Persona layer`: which Korean synthetic persona jobs could explain the same observed scene differently.
- `Copy`: what other stores, brands, or neighborhoods start reproducing.

## Evidence Hierarchy

Use stronger evidence to validate weaker attention signals:

1. Public official data: foot traffic, business density, transport, permits, commercial-area data.
2. Reservation and waiting surfaces: visible availability, queue language, region/category clustering.
3. Map reviews: recent public review language, review counts, photo patterns, complaints, repeat-visit cues.
4. Commerce and retail curation: ranking, display language, category movement, offline store/pop-up activation.
5. Public visual signals: geotags, public posts, Shorts/Reels titles, YouTube vlogs, creator route videos, public comments.

Treat social, YouTube, and geotag signals as weak unless they repeat across place reviews, waiting data, commerce surfaces, or public spatial data. Still include YouTube deliberately for physical-world work, because video reveals route order, entrance scenes, queue language, dwell moments, and what creators consider worth showing.

When NVIDIA Korea persona data is active, use it after the scene is observed. Persona data can generate sharper hypotheses, but it should never be treated as direct evidence that people visited, waited, paid, or converted.

## Spatial Operator Lens

Ask:

- Why does this place need to exist here, not anywhere else?
- What route does it create before and after the visit?
- What physical constraint makes the experience valuable: light, facade, queue, table density, parking, view, sound, smell, packaging, or service flow?
- Is the space beautiful only in photos, or operationally repeatable?
- Can this format move to another neighborhood without losing its reason to exist?

## Lifestyle Pattern Lens

Ask:

- What life anxiety, aspiration, boredom, guilt, or identity need is being materialized?
- What are people trying to prove by going, buying, photographing, or waiting?
- Is this a generational taste, a life-stage behavior, or a reaction to economic pressure?
- What became too tiring, and what became ritualized?
- Which private routine is becoming a public scene?

## Founder and Business Lens

Convert the scene into a business question:

- Is this a product, a channel, a merchandising format, a neighborhood strategy, or a service ritual?
- What job is the customer hiring the space or object to do?
- What would make the demand durable after the photo moment fades?
- What can be packaged into a repeatable operating system?
- Which adjacent category benefits: food, beauty, fashion, home, mobility, fintech, tourism, education, or content/IP?

## Signal Decay

Score durability conservatively:

- `5`: repeated routine, structural route change, supply-side replication, or public data support.
- `4`: repeated reviews/waiting/purchase signals over weeks, but not yet structural.
- `3`: strong visual or social attention with plausible business logic.
- `2`: one-off pop-up, celebrity event, viral post, or seasonal spike.
- `1`: isolated post, paid campaign, or unsupported anecdote.

## Output Rules

For each selected signal, include:

- `Scene`: the concrete behavior observed.
- `Material evidence`: movement, dwell, queue, payment, photo, object, route, or copy.
- `Hidden desire`: what people may be trying to solve or show.
- `Business translation`: how this could become a product, space, service, or content angle.
- `Persona interpretation`: 2-4 Korean persona jobs that would experience the scene differently, when persona data is active.
- `Counter-signal`: what would make the interpretation wrong.
- `Next observation`: one thing to verify in the real world.

Write in Korean by default. Keep claims modest. Say "this suggests" when evidence is interpretive.
